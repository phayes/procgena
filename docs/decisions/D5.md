# D5: configurable world and Biome scale

Status: **mechanism decided by the user (2026-11-24); class numbers and
the scale-aware terrain work are drafts.** The four choices under
"Decisions" are the user's. The numbers in "Scale classes" are starting
points to tune with image examples, not decisions.

## Bottom line

- **The priority (the user, 2026-11-24):** game-sized worlds are the
  requirement. That means Minecraft-like, Valheim-like, and up to the
  large open-world games. The design is optimised for those.
  Earth-sized ("real-life") worlds are a stretch goal. Where supporting
  them is cheap, the design leaves the door open. Where it would add cost,
  complexity or precision work to game scales, it is left out and the
  limit is recorded.
- **Metres stay real. Scale is a choice of landscape lengths, not a
  change of units.** Games shrink distances and heights against the real
  world, and keep characters, buildings and objects at real size. The one
  measured case found is Assassin's Creed Odyssey at about 1:20 against
  Greece. The research suggests distances shrink about 10 to 25 times
  and heights 5 to 10 times, but that is the research's own synthesis,
  not a sourced figure. So assets, road and river widths, the 64 m chunk
  grid, LOD and draw ranges, and masks never scale with the World.
- **A World sets a scale pair, `horizontal` and `vertical`** (decided).
  The pair stretches every landscape wavelength and every height:
  - the Terrain presets' relief layers;
  - the world elevation field E;
  - the World's selector fields;
  - river spacing and border warp.

  Slope is vertical ÷ horizontal, so a pair is validated for slope.
  Terrain presets stay written at one reference scale (today's, scale
  1). **World presets per scale class** (small, medium-small, medium,
  large, real-life) set the pair together with region size, Biome extent
  and E, and a class may override any single field.
- **No fixed landscape length in metres** (decided). Every fade, margin,
  ramp and clearance derives from the World's scale and from the local
  size of what it serves. That local size is the relief's height and
  wavelength, or the feature's actual width. The same holds for areas
  within a World: a small-hills region next to mountains gets small-hills
  fades. This also lowers the region floor, which is about 2 km today,
  so small worlds can have small regions.
- **Huge Biomes come from many regions of one type** (decided). Biome
  extent is set by the World's selector-field wavelengths. Region size is
  chosen for plan cost. A 50 km forest is many "pine forest" regions
  blended at their borders, as D4 already allows. No multi-scale region
  lattice is needed for scale.
- **Real-life limits are recorded, and river size opens a question**
  (decided). Rivers already cross regions through ports, but each region
  sizes its own rivers (4 to 40 m), so a river never widens downstream
  across regions. A World-level, analytic river-size field is opened as
  a design question (open question 1). The other real-life limits are
  recorded, not built.
- **What it blocks:** W0 may start at scale 1 (today's lengths). Its
  World options gain the scale pair and the class presets, and its image
  examples tune the class numbers. A new terrain task, "scale-aware lengths", replaces the
  fixed lengths listed under "Lengths in today's code". D4's region size
  becomes a per-class setting.

**Perceived scale comes from crossing time and view, not from km².**
Games tune a Biome or region to take about 1 to 5 minutes to cross at
run speed (and up to about 10 minutes for the largest regions):

- Minecraft about 1 to 3 minutes;
- Valheim about 1 to 5 minutes;
- Skyrim and Elden Ring about 5 to 6 minutes.

A median real ecoregion takes about two days to walk across. Developers
space content at a minute or two of travel, and lean on landmarks seen
from far away. Run speeds in games (5.6–8.75 m/s) are well above a real
jog of about 3 m/s.
So the scale classes below are chosen by crossing time at a game's run
speed, and by how many Biomes fit in a view distance.

**Real rivers by size:** headwater streams are mostly about 0.3 m wide.
Mid-reach rivers (Strahler orders 4 to 6) are roughly 5 to 50 m wide
(not verified, no table found). The Amazon is 0.7 to 14 km wide.
Game rivers sit at the low end, which today's 4 to 40 m covers.

## Lengths in today's code

Every length sorts into one of four kinds. The kind decides what the
scale pair does to it.

1. **Never scales** (object and player scale). These stay in metres in
   every class:
   - assets and their draw ranges (about 2 km for big trees, about 30 m
     for mushrooms);
   - road width 6 m (narrowing to 4 m) and river width 4–6 m (ports
     ≤ 40 m);
   - the chunk grid: 64 m, doubling per LOD level;
   - masks (0.5 m and 2 m texels) and the 1/256 m fixed point;
   - the road grid (8 m), `EDGE_STEP` (8 m) and plant cells (16 m).

   The user chose to keep road and river widths fixed across classes
   (2026-11-24).
2. **Horizontal landscape lengths** (scale with `horizontal`):
   - E's wavelengths (uplands 40 km, continents 150 km);
   - the World's selector-field wavelengths (Biome extent);
   - relief wavelengths in the Terrain presets (250 m to 3 km);
   - river spacing (500 m) and river density;
   - the border warp (100 m amplitude, 800 m wavelength);
   - gully cell size;
   - valley shaping lengths (wobble 180 m, smoothing 60 m, fall
     smoothing 100 m), where they follow the land rather than the river's
     width.
3. **Vertical lengths** (scale with `vertical`):
   - E's heights (400 m and 120 m);
   - preset heights (1.5 m hills on plains up to 900 m mountains);
   - preset base elevations (20, 50, 120 m).
4. **Fixed floors that become derived** (decided: no fixed landscape
   length). Today these are constants in metres:
   - `FEATURE_CLEARANCE` 65 m (from the 20 m largest half-width plus a
     40 m `BANK_REACH`);
   - `CORNER_CLEARANCE` 195 m and `MIN_EDGE` 390 m;
   - `size` ≥ 5 × `min_edge` (1950 m);
   - edge margins of 200–500 m;
   - `CORNER_RAMP` 500 m, `ROAD_RAMP` 500 m, `FADE_STEP` 64 m,
     `ROAD_MERGE` 300 m, `RIVER_SNAP` 120 m;
   - the planning grid's 25 m cell;
   - the relief fade between unlike neighbours. It is at least the
     margin, and at least the gap between the terrain's mean height and
     the blend's mean, ÷ 0.25. For mountains next to plains the gap is at
     most (412 − 20) m, so the fade is at most about 1.6 km. That is an
     upper bound worked from the formula, not a measurement; it suggests
     a 4 km region is near the smallest that holds that contrast.

   Each will derive from the actual sizes involved: clearances from the
   widths of the features actually present, and fades, margins and ramps
   from the relief heights and wavelengths on each side, after scaling.
   The fade's 0.25 is already a slope, so it shrinks with `vertical` on
   its own; the margins and ramps around it do not yet.

**Limits that stay:**

- `MAX_SIZE` is 50 km, set by the exact i128 site tests. It limits a
  region's lattice spacing, not the World's size.
- `MAX_HEIGHT` is 10 km and `MAX_WAVELENGTH` 1000 km per layer.
- `MAX_OCTAVES` is 12.
- The planning grid grows its cells past 400 000 cells, which is about a
  16 km square region at 25 m. Rivers and roads coarsen beyond that.

## Options considered

1. **One uniform multiplier** on all landscape lengths and heights.
   - Simplest, and slope never changes.
   - It can't make worlds where distances shrink much more than heights,
     or tall-and-wide real-life worlds.
   - It would have to leave objects and widths alone anyway, so it is
     the scale pair with the two numbers forced equal.
   - Rejected.
2. **Hand-made Terrain presets per class**, with no multiplier.
   - Full control.
   - Every Terrain preset is duplicated per class, and the copies drift
     apart.
   - Not chosen. A class may still override single fields.
3. **A scale pair, plus World presets per class (chosen).**
   - One set of Terrain presets serves every class.
   - Slope is validated.
   - The classes are data: World presets in the F3 YAML style.

## Decisions (the user, 2026-11-24)

1. **Mechanism:** a World scale pair `{ horizontal, vertical }` that
   stretches the landscape lengths of every Terrain preset, E and the
   selector fields when they are resolved. Slope is validated.
   - Terrain presets are written at reference scale 1.
   - World presets per class set the pair, region size, Biome extent
     and E.
   - Objects, road and river widths, chunks, LOD and masks never scale.
2. **Small end:** lower the region floor. Every relief fade, margin, ramp
   and clearance must scale with the size of things. The user, verbatim:

   > Everything needs to become more scale aware both in terms of world
   > scale and the scale of various areas etc.

   Confirmed reading: no landscape length is fixed in metres. Each
   derives from the World's scale pair and from the local size of what
   it serves (relief height and wavelength, feature width), within a
   World as well as across Worlds.
3. **Large end:** huge Biome areas come from many neighbouring regions of
   one Biome type. Region size stays a cost setting. No multi-scale
   region lattice for scale. Whether regions should vary in size for
   variety stays with D4.
4. **Real-life:** a stretch goal. Its limits are recorded (below), and
   the World-level river-size field is opened as a design question (open
   question 1).

## Scale classes (draft starting points)

These numbers are guesses to start from. They are tuned with image
examples (W0, W6) and walk-throughs, and validated for slope. Scale 1 is
today's presets. Crossing times assume a game run of about 6–8 m/s.

| Class | Like | `horizontal` | `vertical` | Region size | Biome extent | Highest relief | E wavelengths |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | Minecraft | 0.25 | 0.25 | 0.5–1.5 km, once the floors are derived (see open question 3) | one or two regions, 1–2 km (3–5 min); smaller patches (0.3–1 km) come from Ecotopes | about 250 m | 10 / 37 km |
| Medium-small | Valheim | 0.5 | 0.5 | about 2 km | 0.5–2 km (1–4 min) | about 500 m | 20 / 75 km |
| Medium | Skyrim, BotW, Elden Ring | 1 | 1 | 4 km (today) | 2–5 km (5–10 min) | about 1–1.4 km | 40 / 150 km |
| Large | the largest open worlds; Earth at about 1:3 in distance | 3 | 2 | about 8 km | 5–20 km, over several regions | about 2–3 km | 120 / 450 km |
| Real-life (stretch) | Earth | 10 | 4–5 | about 16 km | 50–250 km, over many regions | 4–9 km | 400 km / 1500 km |

Notes:

- **Equal pairs keep today's slopes.** A larger `horizontal` than
  `vertical` makes land gentler. Steep, blocky land like Minecraft's
  comes from the presets (cliffs, terraces), not from the scale.
- **Octaves follow the stretch.** Stretching a wavelength up removes
  walking-scale detail unless octaves are added. Shrinking it adds detail
  finer than the ground mesh. So a scaled layer's octave count derives
  from its scaled wavelength and a fixed detail floor of a few metres,
  capped at `MAX_OCTAVES`. Large classes cost more per sample.
- **Region size is a cost setting.**
  - Plan time grows with area, and road routing faster than area. The
    G3 step 2 cold benchmarks give about 0.5 s at p50 and 2 s at p95 per
    region, most of it roads. Those regions are a few km² each. A 4 km
    lattice region is several times that area and has not been
    benchmarked.
  - Smaller regions are cheaper each, but there are more of them, and
    more of the land lies near a border.
  - Regions are planned one region beyond the view distance, so very
    large regions plan land long before it is seen.
  - The largest useful size is about 16 km, where the planning grid
    coarsens.
- **Biome extent is the selector fields' wavelength.** It scales with
  `horizontal` by default and can be set on its own.

## Real-life limits (recorded, not built)

- **River size does not grow across regions.** Rivers cross borders
  through agreed ports. But each region's offers are built from that
  region alone (terrain/plan.md: "Outbound offers never depend on inbound
  flow"), so a river entering a region ends there or joins that region's
  own drainage. Its width and flow do not add up downstream. Each
  region's rivers are 4–6 m by preset, at most 40 m per port. There are
  no km-wide rivers. See open question 1.
- **E is slope-bounded** (`max_slope` 0.05). Real ranges at full height
  come from the relief layers' heights (up to `MAX_HEIGHT`), not from E.
- **Large-world coordinates belong to S1.** World positions are f64
  (D1), but Bevy's transforms are f32. That is about 1 mm of drift 10 km
  from the origin, 8 mm at 100 km and 6 cm at 1000 km. D1 already leaves
  the choice to S1 (streaming): a floating origin, or integer grid cells
  plus f32 offsets (the `big_space` crate's approach). Large game worlds
  may feel it at their edges; real-life worlds need it.
- **Plan cost grows with view distance.** Real-life views (tens of km)
  need many regions planned ahead. Far land can use the provisional
  relief-only stage ("Loading by chunk"), but it is still work.

## Open questions

1. **A World-level river-size field** (opened by the user, 2026-11-24).
   Could river width and flow grow downstream across regions, as an
   analytic field that every region reads the same way, as it reads E?
   - It must stay pure and local: no region may read a neighbour's plan
     or inbound flow. It must be closed-form, or bounded per region. The
     user's rule is that terrain stays analytic and fast, with no
     simulation.
   - One direction to explore: a coarse, World-wide "river trunk" layout
     derived from E alone (E is smooth and known everywhere), which ports
     read to size the rivers crossing them.
   - It would help large game worlds too, not only real-life.
   - It would also need `MAX_PORT_WIDTH` and the clearances to follow
     the river's width, which decision 2 already asks for.
2. **The class numbers:** each class's pair, region size, Biome extent
   and E. Tuned by image examples and walk-throughs, after W0.
3. **How low the region floor goes, and fixed widths against small
   regions.** Today `FEATURE_CLEARANCE` = largest half-width (20 m) +
   `BANK_REACH` (40 m) + 5 m = 65 m, `CORNER_CLEARANCE` = 3 × that,
   `MIN_EDGE` = 2 × that, and a region is at least 5 × `MIN_EDGE`.
   Worked from that chain (not measured):
   - **Clearance from the real 6 m width, bank reach still 40 m:**
     3 + 40 + 5 = 48 m, so the floor is about 30 × 48 m ≈ 1.4 km.
   - **Bank reach also scaled at 0.25 (10 m):** 18 m, so about 540 m.
   - **Bank reach 0, widths fixed at 6 m:** about 240 m.

   So with widths fixed (the user's choice), the floor depends mostly on
   whether the bank reach and the factor 5 count as landscape lengths.
   The relief fade between unlike neighbours is the other limit. At the
   small class, two scaled lengths also fall under today's clearance
   floors: river spacing (500 m × 0.25 = 125 m, against `RIVER_SPACING`
   130 m) and the warp wavelength (800 m × 0.25 = 200 m, against the
   260 m minimum). Both floors derive from the clearance, so they move
   with it.

   If regions of about 1 km at the small class need narrower roads and
   rivers, the user's choice of fixed widths would have to be revisited.
   To be measured, with the border and seam suites, at the small class.
4. **"The scale of various areas":** is the local size of each region's
   own Terrain enough? That is today's preset lengths, scaled. Or should
   a World also have a scale field that varies over space, for example
   tighter land near a start area?
5. **Ecotope and Biome geometry:** do Ecotope patch sizes and a Biome's
   border noise scale with `horizontal`, or stay at walking scale? Decide
   in C3 and B1.
6. **E's slope bound under a pair with `vertical` ≠ `horizontal`:** keep
   `max_slope` as a slope, or scale it by `vertical ÷ horizontal`?
7. **Large-world coordinates:** when large game worlds need them. The
   choice (floating origin or grid cells) is S1's, per D1.

## What changes where (not made here, except where marked)

- **ROADMAP:** a D5 row in "Decision gates" and a "For the user" item
  (made with this brief).
- **D4:** region size becomes a per-class World setting under D5 (a note
  made with this brief). D4 still decides the default shape, and the
  medium class's default size after the G3 step 2 benchmarks.
- **W0:** World options gain `scale { horizontal, vertical }`, the class
  presets, and resolving Terrain presets and E at that scale.
- **A new terrain task, "scale-aware lengths"** (the G track, before or
  with W0): derive the lengths in kind 4 from scale and local size; scale
  octaves with wavelength; re-run the border, seam and feature suites at
  the small and large classes. It changes results, so it lands in a churn
  window.
- **ARCHITECTURE "5. World":** the World owns the scale pair (a one-line
  note made with this brief).
