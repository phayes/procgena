## Bottom line

- **The question.** D3 decided that a region is one Biome's whole area,
  so two neighbouring regions never share a Biome. Today's region graph
  cuts the plane into cells of about 4 km and leaves the Biome choice to
  the World. Nothing stops two neighbouring cells from choosing the same
  Biome. D4 decides how that is prevented, while every `RegionId` stays
  computable from the World's options and seed alone, in any build order.
- **The trade-off.** In an endless World you can't have all three of:
  1. Biome areas that follow the selector fields freely, as large as the
     fields make them;
  2. regions you can work out locally, within a bounded distance, with
     stable ids;
  3. neighbours that never share a Biome.

  Merging same-Biome cells gives 1 and 3 but loses 2: a uniform area,
  such as an ocean, has no size limit. Keeping regions bounded gives 2
  and 3, but then large uniform areas must be split into *different*
  Biomes. The question is where that split happens and what it looks like.
- **The options:**
  - **A. One cell, one Biome.** Keep today's graph. Each cell is a
    region, and the Biome choice must differ from every neighbour's.
    No change to the graph, the warp or W4.
  - **B. Cells clustered into Biome regions.** Today's cells become
    building blocks. A coarse layer groups them into larger regions,
    with the same differ-from-neighbours choice at the coarse level.
    - **B1** groups cells by distance to a coarse site, so the shapes
      ignore the Biome choice.
    - **B2** groups them by their first-choice Biome inside fixed blocks,
      so the shapes follow the fields.
  - **C. Regions are the Biome areas.** Same-Biome cells merge without
    limit. This works only for bounded or designer-drawn worlds.
- **Recommendation (for the user to confirm or reject):** build W0 on
  **A** with a ranked, priority-ordered Biome choice. Judge from W0's
  image example whether forced variety at cell scale looks wrong. If it
  does, move to **B1** (or **B2**, if Biome outlines must follow the
  fields). The choice rule and the `RegionId` scheme carry over to B
  unchanged. Keep **C** for designer polygon maps only.
- **Region size** is not fixed here. It waits on the terrain lane's G3
  step 2 cold benchmarks (see "Region size").
- **The user must answer** what "share a Biome" means, whether rule sets
  must supply fallback Biomes, whether Biomes with no cover (ocean) are
  exempt, and what large uniform areas should look like (see "Open
  questions").

## The question

From D3's decisions (user, 2026-11-23):

> A region's irregular outline is exactly where its Biome changes, and
> two neighbouring regions never share a Biome. ... that pass must give
> each region a stable id that can be computed locally, from the World's
> options and seed alone, in any build order.

Why it matters: placement seeds are chained World → region's Biome →
Ecotope → Scatter (D3, question 1). So every region border is a seam in
every Scatter, which the Ecotope border protocol blends. If two
neighbours shared a Biome, the same forest would have a seam down the
middle for no reason. With one Biome per region, every seam is a real
Biome change.

ROADMAP's D4 row also moves world_plan.md's open question 2 (region size
and shape) here.

## Constraints

1. **Stable, local `RegionId`.** A region's id, outline and Biome must be
   pure functions of the World's options and seed. They must be
   computable from a bounded neighbourhood, and the same whatever order
   regions are asked for, on any thread. Seeds (`region_seed(W, role,
   region)`) and every `CopyAddress` carry the `RegionId` (D3).
2. **Any build order.** Nothing may depend on which regions were built
   first (world_plan.md, "Why a field").
3. **Biome choice is field first, with pins** (decided 2026-11-23). Rules
   map E and the World's selector fields (ruggedness, moisture) to Biome
   presets, and a user can pin a region's Biome.
4. **The crate split** (ARCHITECTURE 5.1). The terrain crate owns
   geometry and height and never sees Biomes. It gets each region's
   `TerrainOptions` from a terrain source.
5. **Valid terrain regions.** A `Region`'s `Boundary::Polygon` is a
   simple polygon with no holes. Edges must be at least `MIN_EDGE`
   (390 m) so a crossing fits. A region must be several times (margin +
   taper) wide. `RegionEdge` ids include the end points, so a region *can*
   have two separate runs of border facing the same neighbour.
6. **W3's warped borders.** `warp_side` wiggles each straight side
   between two corners, faded to zero over half a wavelength at each
   corner and seeded from the World seed and the two corner positions.
7. **W4's region cache** (as planned). `terrain(key)` builds each region
   once under a per-key lock, in an LRU cache, keyed by `RegionKey`
   today. `TerrainSource::terrain_options(region: RegionKey, ...)`.
8. **Uniform fields are the common case, not an edge case.** E's layers
   have wavelengths of 40 km (uplands) and 150 km (continents), against
   4 km regions (`RegionGraphOptions::default`). So neighbouring regions
   sample nearly the same field values and will usually *want* the same
   Biome.
9. **W0 has three Biomes.** `procgena_biome` has three empty Biome
   presets today: `plains`, `rolling_hills` and `mountains`. A cell in a
   jittered Voronoi graph usually has about six neighbours, and more where
   corners merged. No rule can always give a region a Biome different
   from six neighbours out of three choices.

## How neighbours are kept different (shared by A and B)

Every bounded option needs a rule that makes neighbouring regions'
Biomes differ, so this part is described once.

- **Ranked candidates.** The rules give each region a ranked list of
  Biomes from the field values at its site, best match first. A pin puts
  its Biome first and alone.
- **A fixed priority per region.** Pinned regions come first. The rest
  are ordered by `region_seed(W, "world.region.priority", id)`, with ties
  broken by `RegionId`. (The key name is an example.)
- **The choice.** A region takes its best candidate that no
  higher-priority neighbour has taken. The lower-priority side of every
  pair always avoids the higher one's Biome, so neighbours always differ.
  This assumes a candidate is left (below).
- **Pure and order-free.** A region's Biome depends on its neighbours of
  higher priority, then on theirs, and so on. That chain is a pure
  function of the options and seed, and cached results can be shared.
- **How far a region looks (argued, not measured).** The chain follows
  only higher-priority neighbours that wanted the same Biome. With random
  priorities, a long chain of rising priorities is rare: the expected
  length is a few steps. **There is no hard bound.** A hard bound is
  possible if priority classes come from a fixed periodic pattern on the
  lattice, so neighbours never share a class. Depth is then at most the
  number of classes, but that bounds the whole neighbourhood to search.
  Whether a hard bound is needed is an open question.
- **When candidates run out.** If every candidate is taken by a
  higher-priority neighbour, the rule fails. It can't be checked ahead of
  time in an endless World. To be safe, every region needs more
  candidates than it has neighbours, so rule sets must pad their lists
  with fallback Biomes (open questions 2 and 3).
- **Two equal pins side by side** are a validation error. Pins are a
  finite list, so this can be checked up front.

## Options

### A. One cell, one Biome

**What it is.** Today's graph, unchanged: one region per staggered
hexagonal lattice cell, with warped borders. The World picks each cell's
Biome with the rule above, so neighbouring cells always differ. Each
Biome area is exactly one cell.

**What it looks like.** Biome areas are all about one region across
(4 km by default, varied by jitter). Where the fields are uniform, the
rule forces alternation between candidates: a mountain range 30 km long
becomes a patchwork of the mountain family's Biomes, each one cell. That
reads well if each family has real siblings ("alpine forest", "alpine
meadow", "scree"). It reads badly with one Biome per family, or for
open ocean.

**Cost in today's code.**

| Piece | Change |
|---|---|
| `graph.rs` | None. A cheap `neighbours(key)` (from the ring, without warping) would help the choice rule; today it must build the warped `Region` |
| `warp.rs` | None |
| W4 region cache | None: keyed by `RegionKey`, as planned |
| `TerrainSource` | None |
| `RegionId` and `region_seed` | D3's packing (`x << 32 \| z`) stands |
| Terrain | None. Cells are simple polygons, and `min_edge` already holds |
| World (W0) | The ranked, priority-ordered choice and its tests |

### B. Cells clustered into Biome regions

**What it is.** Two levels. Today's lattice cells (the **fine cells**)
are grouped into larger **regions**, and each region is one Biome, chosen
by the same rule at the region level. The region's outline is the
outline of its cells, so it keeps their jitter and warp, and it is more
irregular than one cell. Region sizes vary with how many cells a group
holds.

- **B1, grouped by distance.** A coarse jittered lattice (say 3 to 5
  fine cells across) has one site per coarse cell. Each fine cell joins
  the coarse site nearest its own site. The geometry ignores the Biome
  choice, so the terrain crate stays Biome-blind. Uniform areas still
  alternate, at the coarse scale instead of the cell scale.
- **B2, grouped by first choice inside blocks.** Each fine cell takes its
  first-choice Biome from the fields. Connected same-Biome cells inside
  one fixed block (a coarse lattice cell) form a region. Where a region
  meets one in the next block with the same Biome, the rule above makes
  one of them fall back. Outlines follow the fields inside a block, and
  are cut along block lines where the fields are uniform. The geometry
  depends on the first choice, so WorldTerrain needs a **class source**
  next to its terrain source: an opaque class per fine cell, which the
  World derives from its rules. The terrain crate still never sees a
  Biome, but its graph now depends on the World's rules.

**Ids.** A region's id is the `RegionId` of its **anchor cell**, the
smallest `RegionKey` among its cells. Each fine cell lies in exactly one
region, so ids are unique and stable, and D3's packing and `region_seed`
stand unchanged.

**Locality.** Bounded: a region lies within its coarse cell (B1, plus a
fringe from jitter) or its block (B2). The Biome choice has the same
chain depth as A, counted over regions instead of cells.

**Polygon validity.** This is the real cost.

- A union of cells can have a **hole**: a group enclosed by one other
  group. `Boundary::Polygon` has no holes, so either the enclosed group
  is absorbed into the one around it (losing a small patch), or the
  surrounding region is split by a cut. B2 makes holes common: a patch
  of one class inside a sea of another.
- A union can be **disconnected** where jitter puts a fine site in the
  wrong coarse cell (B1), or can touch a neighbour along two separate runs
  (allowed: edge ids include the end points).
- **Region corners** are the fine corners where three or more regions
  meet. A region edge between two of them may be a single short fine
  side, so the `min_edge` merge has to run again at the region level.

**Cost in today's code.**

| Piece | Change |
|---|---|
| `graph.rs` | A grouping layer on top of the rings: groups, outlines as unions, region corners and edges as chains of fine sides, a second short-edge merge, hole and disconnection handling, `region_at` going cell → group. The largest change; the exact-arithmetic ring walk is reused as it is |
| `warp.rs` | Warp a whole chain between two region corners as one side. Warping each fine side as today would pinch the border to zero at every fine corner, every few km. The per-side amplitude bounds must be reworked for chains |
| W4 region cache | Key by `RegionId` (the anchor) instead of `RegionKey`. Regions are larger and vary in size, so plan time and memory vary per entry |
| `TerrainSource` | Takes the region id. B2 also adds the class source |
| `RegionId` and `region_seed` | Unchanged (anchor cell) |
| Terrain | Larger, more concave regions: more border pieces per region, so more cost per height near borders (warped borders are already about 2.7 times straight ones, W3b) |
| World | The same choice rule as A, run over regions. B2 also derives the per-cell class |

### C. Regions are the Biome areas

**What it is.** Each fine cell takes its first-choice Biome, and every
connected set of same-Biome cells is one region, with no cap. Its id is
the anchor cell's. This is the most literal reading of "a region is one
Biome's whole area".

**Why it fails in an endless World.** A connected set of same-Biome cells
has no size limit. An ocean or a continent-wide plain (constraint 8)
makes it as large as the field makes it, without bound. Finding a cell's
region, its outline or its anchor then means a flood fill of unknown
size. That fails constraint 1. No local rule can cap it without
splitting the set, and splitting a set brings back neighbours with the
same Biome. That is B2.

**Where it works.** In bounded worlds: an island, or a designer's polygon
map. Then the whole set of cells or polygons is finite and known up
front, so a flood fill (or the designer's own outlines) gives the
regions. Two touching polygons with the same Biome are merged when the
map loads, or rejected. Designer polygons already need user-given stable
ids (D3), and those are the `RegionId`s. Holes need the same handling as
in B.

**Cost.** Everything in B, plus a global flood fill. That is acceptable
only for bounded maps (world_plan.md open question 4).

## Comparison

| | A. One cell | B1. Clusters by distance | B2. Clusters by first choice | C. Merged areas |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Locality** | Bounded geometry; choice chain unbounded in theory, short in practice (argued) | Bounded (coarse cell); choice chain as A | Bounded (block); choice chain as A | **Unbounded** in an endless World |
| **Neighbours differ by** | The choice rule | The choice rule | The choice rule | Construction |
| **Uniform areas** | Alternate every cell (about 4 km) | Alternate every coarse cell | Cut along block lines | One area, no limit |
| **Shapes** | Warped hexagonal cells, even sizes | Irregular unions, varied sizes | Follow the fields inside blocks | Follow the fields |
| **Holes and short edges** | None new | Rare holes, short region edges | Frequent holes, short region edges | Frequent holes |
| **Crate split** | Kept | Kept | Kept, but the graph needs a class source from the World | Breaks unless the World computes the graph |
| **Pins** | Pin one cell | Pin one region | Pin one region; a pin also fixes its cells' class | Pin an area |
| **graph.rs / warp.rs** | None | Large / chains | Large / chains | Large / chains, plus flood fill |
| **W4 cache key, source** | Unchanged | `RegionId`, new signature | `RegionId`, new signature, class source | As B2 |
| **`RegionId` packing** | Unchanged | Unchanged (anchor) | Unchanged (anchor) | Unchanged (anchor) |

## Region size

**Not fixed here.** It waits on the terrain lane's G3 step 2 cold
benchmarks, running in parallel. features_plan_review.md says that if
Conflict 4 goes to region-level planning, "region size sets how long a
region's plan takes, so D4 should see step 2's cold benchmarks before it
fixes region sizes." It went that way: planning stays per region, with
macro cells only if step 2 calls for them.

What region size affects:

- **Plan time per region.** The figures already measured are these.
  About 0.3 s per 10 km region (556 ms for two, world_plan.md). Road
  routing takes about 25 ms on plains and up to about 1 s in mountains,
  plus 80 to 170 ms for road-grid heights (ARCHITECTURE, "Plan").
  Planning work grows with area, and road routing likely grows faster.
- **The plan-ahead ring.** Regions are planned one region beyond the view
  distance, so larger regions mean more area planned before it is seen,
  and more plan memory in the W4 cache.
- **Biome patch size.** Under every option here, one region is one Biome
  patch, so the plan unit and the Biome patch are the same thing. Large
  Biome areas (say 20 km or more) need large plans, unless plans split
  into macro cells inside a region (Conflict 4, option b). **This is the
  main interaction with G3.** If step 2 shows that cold plans of large
  regions are too slow, either Biome patches stay small (A at about
  4 km), or macro cells come back.
- **How often neighbours want the same Biome.** The smaller regions are
  next to E's 40 to 150 km wavelengths, the more often neighbours ask
  for the same Biome, and the more often the choice rule forces a
  fallback.
- **Border share.** Border length per km² falls as 1 / size. Near borders,
  heights cost more (warped borders are about 2.7 times straight ones),
  and the Ecotope blend zones take a larger share of the land.
- **Limits already in code.** `size` ≥ 5 × `min_edge` (1950 m at the
  default), `size` ≤ `MAX_SIZE` (50 km, for exact i128 arithmetic), and
  "several times (margin + taper)" for each Terrain. The warp default
  (100 m / 800 m) is set for a few-km region.
- **Shape and variety** (world_plan.md open question 2: even regions, or a
  multi-scale mix?). A gives even regions. B1 gives varied ones from the
  number of cells per group. B2 and C give sizes set by the fields. A
  true multi-scale lattice (large regions with occasional small ones) is
  another way to get variety on top of A. It was not worked out here.

## Recommendation (draft, for the user)

**Build W0 on A,** with the ranked, priority-ordered choice and
`RegionId` unchanged.

- It meets every constraint today, and it is the only option that costs
  nothing in G5's graph, warp or W4 cache.
- Its choice rule and its id scheme are exactly what B needs too. Moving
  from A to B later changes the geometry, not the World's choice code or
  D3's seeds.
- W0's image example is the evidence for the next step. If uniform areas
  alternating at cell scale look wrong, move to B1, or to B2 if Biome
  outlines must follow the fields. B1 is the cheaper of the two and keeps
  the graph independent of the World's rules.
- Keep C for bounded and designer-polygon worlds, where a finite flood
  fill (or the designer's outlines) is fine.

**What would change this recommendation:**

- If the user wants Biome areas to follow the fields at their full size
  (open question 4), A is the wrong start: go to B2 directly.
- If G3 step 2 shows that cold plans of regions several times today's
  size are too slow, B's larger regions become expensive, and A stays.
- If measuring W0 shows long choice chains or frequent fallbacks, a hard
  bound (periodic priority classes) or more candidates per family is
  needed first.
- If the user exempts cover-less Biomes (open question 3), ocean no
  longer forces alternation, and A looks much better.

**What W0 must have, in any case:** at least as many candidate Biomes per
region as it has neighbours, or a defined failure (open question 2). Three
empty Biomes can't satisfy the rule everywhere (constraint 9).

## Open questions for the user

1. **What does "share a Biome" mean?** The same Biome preset? Or a Biome
   with the same Ecotopes (two presets that differ only in their Terrain
   would then also count as "the same")? Or the same Terrain preset?
2. **Must rule sets supply fallback Biomes?** Under A and B, a region
   whose best Biome is taken by a neighbour falls back to its next
   candidate. Should each rule name a family of siblings (for example
   "alpine forest", "alpine meadow", "scree")? Or may the rule fall back
   to any Biome, ranked by field distance? And what happens when every
   candidate is taken: a validation error for the rule set (requiring
   enough candidates), or something else?
3. **Are Biomes with no cover exempt?** The seam the rule prevents is a
   *cover* seam (chained Scatter seeds). Two neighbouring regions on the
   same empty Biome (ocean, bare plains) have the same Terrain preset on
   the same World noise, and no Scatters, so there is no visible seam.
   Exempting them would let oceans and bare land stay one Biome. W0 is
   all empty Biomes, so under this exemption it would need no rule at
   all. This narrows D3's rule; it does not reverse it.
4. **What should large uniform areas look like?** A 100 km ocean, or a
   continent-wide plain: one Biome everywhere (only bounded worlds, or the
   exemption in question 3), alternating siblings at a fixed scale (A,
   B1), or field-shaped areas cut along block lines (B2)?
5. **Is a hard bound on the choice chain required?** Or is an expected
   length of a few steps, measured on W0, enough?
6. **Holes** (B and C only): absorb an enclosed patch into the region
   around it, or split the surrounding region with a cut?
7. **Region size and shape:** see "Region size". To be decided after G3
   step 2's cold benchmarks, and after W0's image example if A is chosen.
